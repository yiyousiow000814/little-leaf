## Completion and safe display release

A completed job releases its actual task/resource ownership immediately. A previous display participant can remain only by matching its live node identity. This does not depend on action-name lists, so either completion order and the final local-disposal frame work alike.

Replans start from the currently separated arrangement and retain valid productive contact goals. Departing actors follow their existing logical route. A bounded lookahead reads at most two existing route segments, at most .60 world units; it never finds a route. Analytic swept body/head separation prevents an additional visual catch-up step toward a colleague. A proven continuous contact may settle without chasing an arbitrary grid endpoint. Infeasible geometry is exposed in shared_stance_constraints, not silently collapsed to natural docking.

A cell boundary or fixed distance is not sufficient to release a still-visible offset. The current roster bounds retention, and each actor belongs to at most one current group. Removed/replaced actors, layout holds and changed model identity/revision invalidate the relevant state. Once ordinary drawing is clear, offsets return at the unchanged 1.5 world units/s and the group disappears. There is deliberately no unconditional job-completion timeout: that could force overlap if a peer remains nearby. After an unobstructed zero-offset release becomes valid, the largest permitted offset (.55 on each axis) takes at most sqrt(2)*.55/1.5 = .519 seconds plus a frame to retire. The focused unchanged-route cases retire within 45 frames at .05 s/frame; this is a fixture bound, not a global latency or FPS claim.

## Concrete cost and state bounds

Let N be the current participating roster, at most the model's 11 staff, and W the shared wipers, at most N. Groups are disjoint and have at least two actors, so there are at most floor(N/2) retained groups and N retained participant descriptors. There is no participant history. Furniture references are frame-local and are excluded from retained descriptors and signatures.

- A rebuilt contact domain evaluates at most 32 offsets per actor: 25 grid points, at most five authored-depth tangents, natural docking and the current offset. At most one additional accepted-continuous-contact validation per actor gives 33N full candidate validations per changing frame.
- A next-step solve visits at most 32 cached goals plus one held position per actor. Goal and next-step placement each run exactly two coordinate passes, not recursion or a search tree. At N=11, the two worst-case passes together make at most 14,740 pair-cost comparisons, plus bounded validity checks. Static transit and pathfinding calls: zero.
- Each newly validated wipe offset uses at most eight fixed-arm poses, 24 rounded-cloth vertices and a 64-edge top. There are no 65-sample production loops. Cached unchanged domains reuse those witnesses.
- A changing shared wiper uses at most 34 one-actor next-pose previews in a frame, including a failed settled check. Each preview creates one transient motion object and duplicates exactly three small actor/foot dictionaries; its sample/holder dictionaries and geometry temporaries are also transient. It never copies a service or model dictionary. The observed four-style fixture peaked at six previews in one frame, not the bound.
- The immutable style/contact cache has at most four styles times four rotations, 16 entries; it contains only authored geometry. Script reload invalidates it. Contact domains invalidate on model identity/revision, target/action/position or natural docking changes. The actual-motion resting proof belongs to its current placement and is invalidated when motion changes or that placement is rebuilt.
- The focused test asserts that settled feet produce zero candidate checks, zero next-step candidate evaluations and zero motion previews. It also checks preview immutability and live-actor retention bounds.

These are source/counter bounds, not a measured frame-time or visual-quality claim. Native review must inspect actual bodies, arms, cloth/tools, table supports and walls, including both departure orders and two/three-person scenes.
